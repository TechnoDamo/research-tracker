# Monthly Research Review — 2026-09

## Executive Summary

- A **targeted retrospective search** identified ten relevant September releases: seven directly related to ZO training and three adjacent methods. It was conducted on **6 October**, not during September, and is not an exhaustive monthly scan.
- The strongest topic-level signal is diversification of approaches to ZO efficiency: adaptive step selection, structured perturbation directions, sparse updates, probe-space conditioning, and model sharding. These are separate proposals, not a demonstrated consensus or a common benchmark win.
- [SOMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37899) is the most direct pretraining result, but its highlighted controlled comparison uses 8.44M-parameter LSTM experts and substantial research compute. [SOLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35440) reaches larger transformer pretraining scale through local learning, a different method family.
- None of the identified work independently establishes that pure ZO pretraining matches strong backpropagation baselines at foundation-model scale. Importance and evidential maturity should remain separate in project decisions.

## Top Findings of the Month

1. **SOMA — architectural variance reduction for ZO pretraining.** [Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37899) · [weekly assessment](../weekly/2026-W40.md). The explicit sharding experiment addresses a core ZO bottleneck, but representation sharing and aggregate compute remain concerns.
2. **Probe-space preconditioning — better use of forward evaluations.** [Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38095) · [weekly assessment](../weekly/2026-W40.md). Strongly relevant to the ZO convergence gap; assess total probe and tuning cost rather than step count alone.
3. **AIM-ZO — activation-informed subspaces for LLM fine-tuning.** [Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35257) · [weekly assessment](../weekly/2026-W40.md). Its matched-forward-budget framing and released code make it a useful comparator for structured perturbations.
4. **SOLO — a scale comparison from adjacent local learning.** [Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35440) · [weekly assessment](../weekly/2026-W40.md). Billion-parameter transformer pretraining without global backward dependencies is relevant context, though it is not pure ZO.
5. **ZOCheck — operational reliability for long ZO runs.** [Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27189) · [weekly assessment](../weekly/2026-W39.md). Its update-log design addresses a practical systems cost distinct from optimization quality.

## Important Methodological Developments

**Use more informative directions.** [AIM-ZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35257) maintains an activation-informed direction pool; [probe-space preconditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38095) reweights sampled probes; [TerMeZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33548) uses ternary quantizer geometry to select weights; [directional-hint theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08277) formalizes how to exploit imperfect guidance. Their mechanisms differ, but all target wasted evaluations or ineffective perturbations.

**Adapt steps and training structure.** [MpSub](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07666) replaces a tuned learning rate with a trust-region decision. [SOMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37899) changes the model/objective by independently training experts. [HO-FL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39074) deliberately keeps first-order updates in part of a model. These should not be treated as interchangeable ZO baselines.

**Widen the application surface.** [ComPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19144) studies preference alignment through comparison oracles, while [ZOCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27189) targets fault recovery rather than estimator quality.

## Evidence / Reliability Updates

The month is dominated by first-party preprints. AIM-ZO's arXiv record says it was submitted to ICLR 2027; that is not an acceptance. ZOCheck's arXiv record states SC26 acceptance, which this targeted session did not independently verify against proceedings. Author-linked code was verified for [AIM-ZO](https://github.com/EkkoXy/AIM-ZO), [ZOCheck](https://github.com/DIR-LAB/ZOCheck), and [HO-FL](https://github.com/HKU-WILL-Lab/HO-FL); code availability improves inspectability but is not replication. No independent replication or decisive correction was identified in the session.

Two claims merit particular scrutiny: SOMA's advantage is on small LSTM experts with separable losses and a large research-compute program, while the probe-space paper's dramatic step reduction must be read alongside forward-pass counts, tuning effort, and task protocol. Their potential importance is high; their evidential maturity is still intermediate.

## Emerging Themes

- **Structure over isotropy:** Several proposals direct ZO effort toward promising dimensions or probes. This is a pattern in research questions, not yet a convergent answer on which method wins.
- **Memory savings have different causes:** Pure forward-only ZO, hybrid ZO/first-order training, local learning, and replayable ZO state solve different bottlenecks. Compare memory, wall time, and model quality jointly.
- **Pretraining evidence remains thin:** The month includes one direct ZO pretraining architecture study and one adjacent larger-scale local-learning result. Neither alone resolves transformer-scale pure ZO pretraining.

## What Matters for Our Research

Read SOMA's separable-objective assumptions and full compute accounting, then compare its results with monolithic ZO and backpropagation under the same model and token budget. Reproduce or audit probe-space preconditioning and AIM-ZO under a common **forward-pass and wall-time** budget. Keep SOLO as a comparator for removing global backward dependencies, not as evidence that ZO itself has scaled. Deprioritize generic directional-hint theory until a neural-training experiment connects it to this topic.

This targeted month does **not** justify a material change in research direction. It does identify a concrete evaluation agenda: structured directions, fair compute accounting, and pretraining-scale baselines.

## Recommended Reading

1. [SOMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37899) — direct ZO pretraining and the clearest architecture-level claim.
2. [Probe-space preconditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38095) — test the claimed speed/convergence trade-off carefully.
3. [AIM-ZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35257) — compare activation-informed directions using its released code.
4. [SOLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35440) — adjacent benchmark for larger-scale training without global backpropagation.
5. [ZOCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27189) — systems design if long ZO experiments become practical.
