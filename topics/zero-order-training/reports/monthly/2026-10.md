# Monthly Research Review — 2026-10

## Executive Summary

- **Largest development:** [Dust](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust) made activation-space perturbations a concrete transformer-pretraining candidate; the related [Noise benchmark](https://github.com/qlabs-eng/noise) produced rapid, inspectable optimization progress on a fixed 37.7M-parameter GPT.
- **Crucial distinction:** Noise's 10M-token speedrun moved from 1.76 hours to 1.58 seconds **to a fixed validation-loss threshold** by Oct 22; that is not a 4,000× improvement in general pretraining throughput. Under the 30M-token rules, an Oct 30 fixed-architecture record reached loss 3.300, compared with the benchmark's backprop baselines 3.419 (Adam) and 3.340 (ANVIL II). Both results are first-party and benchmark-specific.
- **Optimizer directions:** SAZO, AdaSub-ZO, StructZO, GRZO's October conference publication and ZO-Muon's Oct 28 revision point to distinct variance-reduction levers: samples, low-rank directions, spectral geometry, and per-example perturbations.
- **Evidence:** Ten distinct works with October release/research-event evidence and three older works with October conference/revision events are indexed below. The month was searched in catch-up sessions; **coverage is partial** and no foundation-model-scale compute-matched ZO parity is established.

## Top Findings of the Month

1. **[Dust](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust)** ([assessment](../weekly/2026-W41.md#zero-order-training-2026-10-06-dust)) — tokenwise activation perturbation is the clearest direct test of learning transformer representations from scratch without backprop. Authors explicitly concede a substantial compute-efficiency gap.
2. **[Noise](https://github.com/qlabs-eng/noise)** ([launch](../weekly/2026-W42.md#zero-order-training-2026-10-24-noise); [Oct 22 update](../weekly/2026-W43.md#zero-order-training-2026-10-24-noise); [Oct 30 update](../weekly/2026-W44.md#zero-order-training-2026-10-24-noise)) — the strongest systems signal: code-backed records, estimator and kernel co-design, and a 30M-token extension. The 10M and 30M score thresholds are not directly comparable.
3. **[StructZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16451)** ([assessment](../weekly/2026-W42.md#zero-order-training-2026-10-24-structzo)) — combines activation-informed subspaces and spectral control, reporting near-FO quality on evaluated fine-tuning tasks but requiring a forward-query and wall-time audit.
4. **[GRZO](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1711/)** ([October conference assessment](../weekly/2026-W44.md#zero-order-training-2026-11-02-grzo)) — per-example pseudo-independent perturbations and group-relative normalization with two model forwards. First released as a June preprint; October is its EMNLP proceedings event.
5. **[ZO-Muon v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17155v3)** ([revision assessment](../weekly/2026-W44.md#zero-order-training-2026-11-02-zo-muon)) — low-rank projected ZO updates with spectral orthogonalization; the Oct 28 revision expands its claimed vision-model scope.

## Important Methodological Developments

**Credit assignment and systems optimization.** [Dust](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust) replaces weight-space populations with per-token activation perturbations; [Noise](https://github.com/qlabs-eng/noise) rapidly explored deterministic Hadamard cycles, identity-path credit, future-token probes, fast kernels and optimizer co-design. The 30M open-architecture record of 3.211 is a different architecture track and cannot be used as a fixed-model victory. [Official PR #54](https://github.com/qlabs-eng/noise/pull/54) reports fixed-architecture 3.2998 on seed 0 and a research five-seed mean of 3.3014; stronger independent verification and compute normalization are still required.

**Estimator variance is attacked along different axes.** [SAZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12566) reallocates queries among examples; [AdaSub-ZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15225) learns low-rank directions; [StructZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16451) uses activation geometry and spectral control; [GRZO](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1711/) uses batchwise perturbations; [ZO-Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17155v3) orthogonalizes subspace estimates. These are not interchangeable methods or an established ranking under matched GPU-hours.

**Different meanings of backprop-free.** [ZFO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02190) uses first-order gradients to choose directions and ZO evaluations only for step length. [PotentialEP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14409) uses equilibrium propagation on small generative networks, not standard random-direction ZO. [ZeroQAT](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.28/) focuses on low-bit adaptation; [federated ZO unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35211) focuses on exact distributional deletion. [Wavelet CMA-ES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29697) uses a specialized compact reconstruction architecture. None directly resolves general LLM pretraining scaling.

## Emerging Themes

The strongest hypothesis worth testing is that **credit-assignment structure** and **estimator–optimizer–hardware co-design** matter at least as much as the number of random perturbations. A separate but compatible hypothesis is that batchwise variance reduction and spectral conditioning can reduce the number of useful forward queries needed. Neither hypothesis has a robust compute-matched pretraining comparison across model sizes.

## What Matters for Our Research

For hybrid FO/ZO pretraining, retain the strong FO AdamW reference and match total GPU time, token count, model architecture and tuning budgets. Compare FO head/embedding updates against pure ZO with at least one structured-direction baseline (StructZO/ZO-Muon-like) and one per-example estimator (GRZO-like). Consider a small-scale Dust-style activation-perturbation pilot, but do not substitute Noise leaderboard times for pretraining efficiency. Measure update-direction cosine, forward-equivalent FLOPs, memory, and quality across seeds. Treat the Noise open-architecture track as a separate research question.
