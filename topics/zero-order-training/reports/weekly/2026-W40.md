# Weekly Research Review — 2026-W40

## Executive Summary

- **Meaningful new findings identified:** 5 releases dated 28–30 September: three direct ZO contributions and two adjacent training approaches.
- **Most important finding:** SOMA tests model sharding for ZO pretraining and reports better compute efficiency than its monolithic ZO controls, but on small LSTM experts at high research compute cost.
- **Other important developments:** Probe-space preconditioning and activation-informed subspaces target ZO update efficiency; SOLO explores local learning at billion-parameter pretraining scale.
- **Evidence and direction:** These are largely first-party preprints. They suggest several promising routes but do not establish that ZO pretraining matches strong backpropagation baselines at foundation-model scale.
- **Coverage:** The ISO week runs through 4 October; this September backfill covers **28–30 September only**.

## High Priority — directly relevant

### zero-order-training-2026-10-06-soma — Scaling Zero-Order Pretraining through Model Sharding

- **Authors:** Francois Chaubard, Mykel J. Kochenderfer, Chris Ré.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-09-29.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); a corresponding ICLR 2027 OpenReview submission was found, with no decision verified.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37899
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** Sharded Optimization Mixture of Assemblies (SOMA) independently trains LSTM experts on data clusters with SPSA and separable losses. The authors report lower test loss than monolithic SPSA and EGGROLL controls at an 8.44M-parameter, 150 aggregate GPU-hour comparison and analyze a roughly `1/N` relative-gradient-variance reduction for a fixed separable objective.
- **Why it matters:** It tests an architectural way to reduce the dimension and variance problem that obstructs ZO pretraining, rather than only changing the estimator.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original 38-page preprint with controlled comparisons, a stated 80,000 estimated RTX 5090 GPU-hour research program, and a theoretical result for a separable objective. The claims and compute accounting are author-reported; the OpenReview submission is not an acceptance.
- **Major limitations:** The highlighted controlled training result uses small LSTM experts, not transformer foundation models. Separation sacrifices jointly learned representations; larger ensembles can require far more aggregate training compute. Independent replication was not identified.
- **Code / project URL:** Authors say code and checkpoints are released, but no stable official link was verified in this search.
- **Status / update:** New arXiv preprint, catalogued retrospectively on 2026-10-06; alternate OpenReview submission deduplicated.

### zero-order-training-2026-10-06-probe-space — Probe-Space Preconditioning for Fast and Stable Zero-Order Training

- **Authors:** Francois Chaubard, Mykel J. Kochenderfer, Chris Ré.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-09-29.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); preprint.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38095
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** The paper argues for larger effective probe batches under fixed compute and introduces 1.5-SPSA, which adds a clean forward pass to estimate a diagonal preconditioner in probe space. The authors report stronger post-training results on Qwen3 and OPT tasks and demonstrate in-place optimization up to OPT-30B.
- **Why it matters:** Probe allocation and conditioning are central to whether ZO can close the convergence gap at tolerable compute and memory cost.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original 16-page preprint with six post-training datasets and first-party comparisons. Its OPT-13B SST-2 result is reported as 3.1 percentage points above MeZO and backpropagation in 70 steps, but the comparison needs full inspection of total probes, tuning, and task protocol.
- **Major limitations:** The highlighted evidence is post-training, not pretraining. One task result and step counts alone do not establish general compute superiority or quality parity.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** New preprint, catalogued retrospectively on 2026-10-06.

### zero-order-training-2026-10-06-aim-zo — AIM-ZO: Activation-Informed Subspace Maintenance for Zeroth-Order LLM Fine-Tuning

- **Authors:** Yue Xie, Zhi Zheng, Yunpeng Ba, Xuyang Wu, Xialiang Tong, Zhichao Lu, Tao Zhong, Zhenkun Wang.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-09-28.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); arXiv comments say submitted to ICLR 2027, with no acceptance decision verified.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35257
- **Importance:** 3/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** AIM-ZO maintains an activation-informed subspace while activating only a subset of directions for each low-dimensional ZO perturbation. The authors report tests across five LLMs and eleven downstream tasks under matched forward-evaluation budgets, including a 1.26-point six-task average gain over their strongest fully evaluated ZO baseline on OPT-2.7B.
- **Why it matters:** It directly attacks wasted evaluations from isotropic perturbations while retaining a broader pool of informative directions.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original preprint with a stated matched-budget comparison and an [author-linked code repository](https://github.com/EkkoXy/AIM-ZO). Results remain first-party; submission to ICLR 2027 does not imply acceptance.
- **Major limitations:** Full compute, memory, and baseline details need close reading; the result addresses fine-tuning rather than pretraining and has no independent replication identified here.
- **Code / project URL:** https://github.com/EkkoXy/AIM-ZO
- **Status / update:** New preprint, catalogued retrospectively on 2026-10-06.

## Medium Priority — useful adjacent work

### zero-order-training-2026-10-06-solo — SOLO: Pretraining Billion-Parameter Language Models with Shared-Output Local Learning

- **Authors:** Bojian Yin, Shurong Wang, Yuqi Pan, Guoqi Li.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-09-28.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); preprint.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35440
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** medium.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** SOLO shares a read-only copy of the final module's output readout with locally trained modules, avoiding global backward dependencies. The authors report transformer pretraining at 340M–2B parameters on 15B tokens, zero-shot accuracy within one point of their backpropagation baseline, and up to 1.44× measured throughput on a matched pipeline partition.
- **Why it matters:** It is a useful adjacent comparison for memory-efficient training without end-to-end backpropagation, especially at a larger scale than the ZO pretraining papers in this set.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original 26-page first-party preprint with ablations and throughput comparisons. It is **local learning**, not a pure zeroth-order parameter-perturbation method.
- **Major limitations:** Do not generalize its gains to ZO. The reported throughput depends on pipeline setup; quality and compute trade-offs need full-paper scrutiny and independent replication.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** New preprint, catalogued retrospectively on 2026-10-06.

### zero-order-training-2026-10-06-ho-fl — HO-FL: Hybrid-Order Federated Learning for Heterogeneous Edge Devices

- **Authors:** Qiyuan Chen, Xian Wu, Yanan Ma, Xianhao Chen.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-09-30.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); preprint.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39074
- **Importance:** 3/5.
- **Relevance:** medium.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** HO-FL lets edge devices train lower model segments with ZO updates and upper segments with first-order updates, choosing the boundary by memory budget. The authors analyze a client-selection trade-off and report language-task experiments that retain much of full first-order performance at lower client memory.
- **Why it matters:** Hybrid-order training may be a practical compromise when fully ZO training converges too slowly under heterogeneous memory limits.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original preprint with theory, first-party experiments, and an [author-linked code repository](https://github.com/HKU-WILL-Lab/HO-FL).
- **Major limitations:** It requires backpropagation in part of the model and studies federated edge settings, so it does not answer whether pure ZO pretraining scales.
- **Code / project URL:** https://github.com/HKU-WILL-Lab/HO-FL
- **Status / update:** New preprint, catalogued retrospectively on 2026-10-06.
