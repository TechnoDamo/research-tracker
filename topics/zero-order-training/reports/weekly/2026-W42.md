## High Priority — directly relevant

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-12-zfo"></span>[Trust the Direction, Search the Step: Zero-and-First-Order Methods for LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02190)

- **Authors:** Cristian McGee, El Houcine Bergou, Aritra Dutta.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-01.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); the arXiv record reports NeurIPS 2026 acceptance, but this scan did not independently match the exact title to an official conference program/proceedings record.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02190
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** ZFO decouples update direction from step length: a trusted first-order optimizer proposes the direction, then the method uses the current gradient information plus two additional objective evaluations along that one-dimensional subspace to fit a local model and choose a curvature-aware step. The authors provide convergence analysis and report frequent optimization/final-performance improvements over fixed-step first-order baselines across evaluated language-model settings.
- **Why it matters:** It is a direct hybrid FO/ZO design showing where zeroth-order evaluations may add value even when fully replacing gradients is unnecessary or too expensive. For hybrid-method research, it isolates a concrete role for function evaluations: step selection rather than full high-dimensional gradient estimation.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original arXiv preprint with theory, language-model experiments, and public code. The method uses first-order gradients for the search direction, so its compute and memory profile is fundamentally different from forward-only MeZO/Dust-style training.
- **Major limitations:** It is not backpropagation-free and therefore does not solve the activation-memory problem motivating pure ZO training. Full baseline tuning and total extra-evaluation cost need close inspection. The reported NeurIPS status was not independently verified from an official conference record in this scan.
- **Code / project URL:** https://github.com/nizswan/Zeroth-First-Order-Framework
- **Status / update:** Late discovery. Released 2026-10-01 but first observed during ordinary monitoring on 2026-10-12, so it belongs to W42's observation week rather than being rewritten into W40.

## Medium Priority — useful adjacent work

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-12-async-es"></span>[Asynchronous Is Nearly Free for Evolution Strategies on Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04196)

- **Authors:** William Hoy, Jingxuan Fan, Nurcin Celik, Xu Pan.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-03.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); short-form preprint.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04196
- **Importance:** 3/5.
- **Relevance:** medium.
- **Evidential maturity:** 2/5.
- **Contribution:** The paper introduces bounded-staleness asynchronous ES for LLM agentic post-training. On Endless Terminals with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the authors report Async-1 at 25.9% held-out success versus 25.4% for synchronous ES across three evaluation seeds; controlled four- and eight-update delays on 10% of a cohort reduce success by 1.6 and 3.1 points respectively.
- **Why it matters:** ES/ZO training can require many expensive rollouts or forward evaluations; removing synchronization barriers could reduce wasted wall time without changing the estimator itself.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original short-form arXiv paper with a direct sync-versus-async experiment. The same evaluation reports GRPO at 29.0%, so the result supports asynchronous feasibility rather than superiority of ES over gradient-based post-training.
- **Major limitations:** One model, one agentic benchmark, post-training rather than pretraining, and limited independent scrutiny. Moderate staleness tolerance does not establish scaling behavior for parameter-perturbation pretraining.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** Late discovery. Released 2026-10-03 and first observed on 2026-10-12, so it is recorded in W42 under the ongoing-monitoring observation rule.
