## High Priority — directly relevant

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-24-noise"></span>[Noise: Speedrun for Pretraining Without Backpropagation](https://qlabs.sh/noise)

- **Authors:** Serdar Gülbahar, Bishwas Mandal, Akshay Vegesna, Samip Dahal.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-12.
- **Source / venue:** Q Labs Research and official project repository.
- **Publication type:** project_release.
- **Canonical URL:** https://qlabs.sh/noise
- **Importance:** 5/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 2/5.
- **Contribution:** Noise fixes Dust's 37.7M-parameter transformer, FineWeb training data, validation score, and one 8×H100 node, then opens two no-backprop tracks: lowest validation loss and fastest time to a target loss. At launch, Dust with Adam was the baseline at 4.167 validation loss and 1.76 hours to the speed target.
- **Why it matters:** It creates a reproducible competitive arena for estimator/optimizer co-design in backprop-free pretraining, directly aligned with this tracker’s core question.
- **Evidence and source quality:** First-party benchmark with public rules and code. Records are inspectable project submissions rather than independent scientific replications.
- **Major limitations:** Fixed small model/data regime; benchmark optimization can overfit the setting, and scaling to larger models/tokens remains unproven. Record changes after 19 October are intentionally excluded from this W42 report.
- **Code / project URL:** https://github.com/qlabs-eng/noise
- **Status / update:** New W42 project release discovered in the Oct 24 catch-up.

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-24-structzo"></span>[StructZO: Structured Zeroth-Order Optimization for Memory-Efficient LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16451)

- **Authors:** Xuanlin Yang, Jia Li, Zhouchen Lin.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-12.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16451
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 2/5.
- **Contribution:** StructZO performs large-query ZO estimation in an activation-induced informative subspace, applies spectral control in reduced space, then maps the update back to parameters. The paper reports up to 8.7% relative average improvement over its strongest ZO baselines and average performance within 1.5% of FO-Adam across evaluated OPT and Llama3.1-8B downstream tasks.
- **Why it matters:** It combines two research directions central to current ZO work: activation-informed subspaces and spectral conditioning, directly relevant to reducing effective dimension and noisy-direction quality.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original 20-page arXiv preprint with theory and LLM experiments; no independent replication or reviewed venue was verified.
- **Major limitations:** Fine-tuning rather than pretraining; large-query estimation cost and wall-time accounting require close audit before treating the near-FO result as an efficiency win.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** New W42 finding discovered in the Oct 24 catch-up.

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-12-zfo"></span>[Trust the Direction, Search the Step: Zero-and-First-Order Methods for LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02190)

- **Authors:** Cristian McGee, El Houcine Bergou, Aritra Dutta.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-01.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02190
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** ZFO uses a first-order optimizer for direction selection and two additional objective evaluations along that direction to choose a curvature-aware step.
- **Why it matters:** It isolates a practical hybrid role for zeroth-order evaluations without requiring them to estimate the full high-dimensional direction.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original arXiv paper with theory, LLM experiments, and public code.
- **Major limitations:** Not backpropagation-free and therefore does not solve activation-memory constraints of pure ZO training.
- **Code / project URL:** https://github.com/nizswan/Zeroth-First-Order-Framework
- **Status / update:** Existing W42 late-discovery finding; retained after catch-up.

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-12-async-es"></span>[Asynchronous Is Nearly Free for Evolution Strategies on Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04196)

- **Authors:** William Hoy, Jingxuan Fan, Nurcin Celik, Xu Pan.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-03.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04196
- **Importance:** 3/5.
- **Relevance:** medium.
- **Evidential maturity:** 2/5.
- **Contribution:** Introduces bounded-staleness asynchronous ES for LLM agentic post-training and reports near-synchronous performance in one benchmark while reducing synchronization pressure.
- **Why it matters:** ES/ZO methods can be rollout-heavy; asynchronous execution may improve wall-clock efficiency without changing the estimator.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original short-form arXiv experiment.
- **Major limitations:** One model, one agentic benchmark, post-training rather than pretraining.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** Existing finding; retained after catch-up.
