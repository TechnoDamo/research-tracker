# Weekly Research Review — 2026-W37 (Sep 7 – Sep 13)

## Executive Summary

- **Meaningful new findings identified:** 2 September preprints: one direct LLM fine-tuning method and one adjacent optimization result.
- **Most important finding:** MpSub replaces a tuned learning rate with an adaptive trust-region radius for forward-only LLM fine-tuning.
- **Evidence and direction:** Its small-model, single-task comparison is useful but does not establish broad superiority over MeZO or first-order training.
- **Coverage:** Retrospective targeted search performed on 6 October; not a full September monitoring scan.

## High Priority — directly relevant

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-06-mpsub"></span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07666" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">MpSub: A Momentum <em>p</em>-Dimensional Subspace Trust-Region Method for Derivative-Free Fine-Tuning of Large Language Models ↗</a>

- **Authors:** Yuyang Wang, Haoyu Yao, Pengcheng Xie.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-09-07.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); preprint.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07666
- **Importance:** 3/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** MpSub combines a momentum direction with fresh random directions in a small subspace and adapts a trust-region radius using observed loss reduction, removing the learning-rate hyperparameter. Its authors report CommitmentBank fine-tuning of OPT-125M and OPT-350M under 8,400 training-objective forward passes, with three-seed mean accuracies of 0.673 and 0.690, comparable to a tuned MeZO baseline.
- **Why it matters:** Learning-rate sensitivity is a practical weakness of ZO fine-tuning; an adaptive step mechanism could lower tuning cost.
- **Evidence and source quality:** The original preprint provides an analysis for smooth deterministic objectives and a matched forward-pass experiment. The empirical claim is first-party and limited to the stated models and task; the theoretical assumptions are narrower than general LLM training.
- **Major limitations:** Only two sub-billion-parameter OPT models and one downstream task are highlighted; a broader compute and task comparison is needed. No independent replication was found in this search.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** New preprint, catalogued retrospectively on 2026-10-06.

## Medium Priority — useful adjacent work

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-06-directional-hints"></span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08277" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Adaptively Incorporating Directional Hints into Zeroth-Order Optimization ↗</a>

- **Authors:** Alexander Ryabchenko, Jian Qian, Wenlong Mou.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-09-08.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv (`arxiv`); preprint.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08277
- **Importance:** 2/5.
- **Relevance:** medium.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** Control-Variate Zeroth-Order Descent adapts to cheap, imperfect directional hints without needing their quality in advance. The authors give a convergence bound that interpolates with hint quality and demonstrate it on simulation-based optimization tasks.
- **Why it matters:** Activation- or subspace-informed perturbations in neural training face the same question of how to use hints without trusting them blindly.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original theoretical preprint with empirical simulations; no neural-network training result is reported in the abstract.
- **Major limitations:** Transfer to LLM fine-tuning or pretraining is untested here, so this is a method idea rather than evidence of improved ZO training.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** New preprint, catalogued retrospectively on 2026-10-06.
