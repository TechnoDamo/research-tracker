## High Priority — directly relevant

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-06-dust"></span>[Dust: Pretraining Transformers Without Backpropagation](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust)

- **Authors:** Samip Dahal, Bishwas Mandal, Serdar Gülbahar, Akshay Vegesna.
- **Publication/release date:** Unknown; Q Labs states October 2026, but the exact day was not verified.
- **Source / venue:** Q Labs Research; first-party lab release.
- **Publication type:** technical_report.
- **Canonical URL:** https://qlabs.sh/research/dust
- **Importance:** 5/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** Dust perturbs activations independently at each token so one forward pass evaluates a large virtual population, then turns reward-weighted perturbations into layer updates. Q Labs reports that sufficiently large populations can approach or sometimes beat its backpropagation baselines.
- **Why it matters:** It directly tests whether zeroth-order training can scale from fine-tuning toward transformer pretraining from scratch.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Detailed first-party methods, FineWeb experiments, multiple seeds in main cells, model-scale tests, and public code. The authors explicitly state that current compute efficiency is not sufficient to replace backpropagation.
- **Major limitations:** Strongest claims require very large populations and some extrapolation; no independent replication or peer-reviewed publication was verified.
- **Code / project URL:** https://github.com/qlabs-eng/dust
- **Status / update:** Existing finding; retained after catch-up.

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-24-sazo"></span>[Sample-Adaptive Zeroth-Order Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12566)

- **Authors:** Haiyuan Wan, Yanyi Li, Weicheng Lin, Cong Fang.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-08.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12566
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 2/5.
- **Contribution:** SAZO estimates evolving sample importance from directional finite differences and reallocates queries without extra model evaluations; the paper reports up to 6× faster convergence to the same training loss across four model families.
- **Why it matters:** It attacks a different variance source than direction design: which training samples receive the scarce ZO query budget.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original arXiv paper with theory and multi-model experiments; no independent replication or reviewed venue was verified.
- **Major limitations:** Post-training rather than pretraining; headline speed is query-count convergence and requires close wall-time/baseline audit.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** Late-discovered W41 release found during the Oct 24 catch-up.

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-24-adasub-zo"></span>[Zeroth-Order Fine-Tuning of LLMs in Learnable Low-Rank Subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15225)

- **Authors:** Chuang Tong, Ling Chen.
- **Publication/release date:** 2026-10-10.
- **Source / venue:** arXiv.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15225
- **Importance:** 4/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 2/5.
- **Contribution:** AdaSub-ZO learns layer-wise low-rank projection matrices directly from finite-difference feedback and adds subspace momentum, replacing fixed random subspaces with task-adaptive ones.
- **Why it matters:** This directly targets effective dimensionality and direction quality, central bottlenecks for ZO LLM optimization.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Original seven-page arXiv preprint with experiments on NLU tasks; no independent replication or peer-review status verified.
- **Major limitations:** Fine-tuning only; empirical scope and compute accounting need deeper audit.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** Late-discovered W41 release found during the Oct 24 catch-up.
