## High Priority — directly relevant

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-06-dust"></span>[Dust: Pretraining Transformers Without Backpropagation](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust)

- **Authors:** Samip Dahal, Bishwas Mandal, Serdar Gülbahar, Akshay Vegesna.
- **Publication/release date:** Unknown; Q Labs states `October 2026`, but the exact day was not verified.
- **Source / venue:** Q Labs Research (`q-labs-research`); first-party lab research release.
- **Publication type:** technical_report.
- **Canonical URL:** https://qlabs.sh/research/dust
- **Importance:** 5/5.
- **Relevance:** high.
- **Evidential maturity:** 3/5.
- **Contribution:** Dust perturbs transformer activations independently at each token so a forward pass evaluates a large virtual population, then converts reward-weighted activation perturbations into layer updates. Q Labs reports that sufficiently large populations can approach or sometimes beat its backpropagation baselines in transformer pretraining and are far more efficient than its weight-space EGGROLL implementation.
- **Why it matters:** This is unusually direct evidence on the project's central question: whether zeroth-order training can scale beyond fine-tuning to representation learning from scratch. It also suggests activation space and population size may be more important design axes than parameter-space dimensionality alone.
- **Evidence and source quality:** The release gives a detailed method, appendices, FineWeb pretraining experiments, separately tuned baselines, three seeds per main cell, and model-size tests from roughly 2M to 243M parameters. The base setup is a 37.7M-parameter transformer. Official code is released and reproduces the estimator/tuned defaults, although the repository says it omits execution optimizations from the full experiments. The authors themselves state that Dust is not compute-efficient enough to replace backpropagation today.
- **Major limitations:** The strongest parity/superiority claims rely on very large populations and, in places, extrapolation. The main training comparisons are much shorter than frontier pretraining; the report's 1B-token result concerns gradient alignment rather than a 1B-token Dust training run. No peer-reviewed venue or independent replication was verified, and the exact public release day remains unknown.
- **Code / project URL:** https://github.com/qlabs-eng/dust
- **Status / update:** New W41 finding. First observed on 2026-10-06; it was left out of the September retrospective only because the page was dated October, then fully assessed in the missed-run retry on 2026-10-12.

## Medium Priority — useful adjacent work

### <span id="zero-order-training-2026-10-06-lifty"></span>[Principled Backpropagation-Free Training via Layerwise Fenchel-Young Lifting](https://stop.sciencesconf.org/758078)

- **Authors:** Diego Pinto-Suárez, Jordan Patracone, Marc Sebban.
- **Publication/release date:** Unknown; author and institutional records identify a 2026 preprint, but an exact public release day was not verified.
- **Source / venue:** 2026 preprint listed by the authors and Institut d'Optique; method abstract also presented at STructured OPtimization for inverse and learning problems.
- **Publication type:** preprint.
- **Canonical URL:** https://stop.sciencesconf.org/758078
- **Importance:** 3/5.
- **Relevance:** medium.
- **Evidential maturity:** 2/5.
- **Contribution:** The work treats intermediate activations as optimization variables and characterizes network composition through vanishing Fenchel-Young gaps. It proposes Lifty, a layerwise backpropagation-free algorithm with closed-form updates for a class of monotone activations and convex regularizers, and states linear convergence to stationary points under its assumptions.
- **Why it matters:** It is a different route to removing the global backward pass and therefore provides a useful comparator for pure perturbation-based ZO methods, especially when thinking about memory, credit assignment, and layer-local optimization.
- **Evidence and source quality:** Authors, preprint status, and the core method are supported by author/institutional listings and an original workshop abstract. The available abstract states theoretical and generalization claims, but this scan did not verify a full foundation-model experiment or an independently reviewed publication record.
- **Major limitations:** This is not a standard ZO perturbation estimator, and no LLM/pretraining-scale result was verified. The full empirical scope, exact release date, and OpenReview venue metadata remain uncertain.
- **Code / project URL:** None verified.
- **Status / update:** Follow-up from the 2026-10-06 session resolved into a W41 finding during the 2026-10-12 retry.
